# Stock and crypto trading

Use this process for market reads, selection, and entries and exits. Every conclusion must be a falsifiable if-then. Do not publish one sacred price as a forecast.

Add this line by default: framework sketch, not a promise of profit.

## Rules

Answer three questions every time. Skip none of them:

1. **What to buy** — only the one or two names that are strongest now, still have range, and whose thesis is not dead.
2. **When to buy** — a good cost plus a signal the market has already given. Do not open in the middle of the range or in a chop zone.
3. **When to sell** — write the exit tree before the session. The only judge of taking profit or stopping out is market structure, not a headline and not a later look back at the price.

Choosing is easy. Acting is hard. Once the plan is written, execute it: open the planned size in one step, lose at the planned stop, take profit at the planned exit. Do not nibble and then chase. That ruins the cost.

Constraints:

- Respect the market. Do not guess the top or the bottom. Let price tell you when to enter and exit.
- Watch few names at once. No movement means no trade. Stay in cash and wait for the next thesis.
- The buy logic and the sell logic must match. Test the idea with size, not with talk.
- Leave when the trend breaks. Hoping turns a small scrape into a large loss.
- Following the plan beats the last bit of profit at the high.
- Look at less news while in a position. News slows the decision.
- A view may change, but write which old thesis the price has falsified.
- Indicators are a shadow of price. In the end, look at price, structure, volume, and relative strength.

## Read the market

### Name the regime before acting

Pick one. Do not hedge the label:

| Regime | Definition | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Trend | Higher highs, or lower lows | Hold while it holds. Leave on a break. |
| Rotation | The index need not rise. Money is moving between sectors. | Trade the leaders, not the index itself. |
| Range | The band is clear and the swings are getting smaller. | Keep a core and grid the noise, or stay in cash. |
| Event campaign | A rate decision, earnings, an unlock, an index inclusion, and the like. | Split the script by day. Buy the expectation, sell the fact. |

A decline does not reverse at the first low. Wait for the range to confirm. Do not buy the dip on the way down. Wait for the right side, or for the most panicked push to fail to break the prior low.

### Order of the read

1. Does the bellwether index give a direction? For US stocks, look at the Nasdaq first.
2. Do the overnight session, overseas markets, the pre-market, and the last half hour of the day lead the regular session?
3. Are the indexes in sync? One index at a new high while the others lag means capital is choosing. A broad rally is then unlikely.
4. How sectors and names sit versus the index. Handle the weak leaders first.
5. Keep one macro story you actually believe, and use it to explain the tape. Do not use it as the trigger to open or close. When it conflicts with price, follow price.

### Questions at a new high or a break

- Did buying follow the new high? If not, few people want this price.
- How deep was the selloff, and how strong were sellers into the close? A shallow dip that is bought back means sellers are not strong either.
- Did the breakout come on shrinking volume, and was disagreement small? Shrinkage with little disagreement is more likely to continue, but the open can still retest.
- Is it a broad rally? A new index high with many stocks unmoved means hold only the leaders.
- Turnover falling from extremely high to extremely low, with both sides accepting the price, is a consensus shelf.
- The most dangerous window failing to break the prior low means selling pressure may be exhausted.
- Are rates, commodities, and the rest of the macro tape one-way or choppy? If they chop, do not use macro as an on/off switch.
- What force pushed the new high? If that force is outside your control and is likely to stay against you, treat the new high as fragile, not as an automatic continuation.
- Is new money flooding the whole market, or only a few high-growth, high-profit names? After the flow narrows, if fresh money does not keep up, the tape gaps. A new index high can then be a reason to cut, or to flip from long toward short.
- In a trend, do not explain a decline with one bearish headline. A decline is first a choice of capital and a structure. The headline is a side dish.

Sentiment, story, and capital all have to come back to buying and selling, which is supply and demand.

At a high, any piece of bad news can crash the price. At a low, any piece of good news can spike it. That is emotional elasticity, not proof of a new trend. Do not expect it in one step.

## Read a stock or coin

Use this order. Do not start from the story and work backward:

1. **Relative strength** — strength stays strong. Names you hold that are not rising should be rotated out before the index makes a new high. Do not wait for the weak to become strong.
2. **Price versus thesis** — a good location is a thesis that is still alive and a price that emotion or macro has already knocked down. If the thesis is dead, cheap is not a reason to hold.
3. **Range** — movement is the trader's food. If the price is lifeless, stop.
4. **Events and trapped supply** — write unlocks, index inclusion, rate decisions, earnings, and round numbers into the script. Supply that is underwater becomes fuel the other way. Count both the supply people feel and the supply that is real.
5. **The sector can come before the name** — if you understand the sector, trade the sector tool. Do not force a story stock.
6. **Direction follows structure** — the same name can be long or short. Focus is not a permanent long or a permanent short.

Avoid the middle of the range, story names that no longer move, a location with no invalidation level, and a dip that has not been confirmed.

You understand a name when it leads its index and its sector, when something larger still pulls it after the event lands, and when this price is one a person can sit with. If they cannot sit with it, the cost is bad or the thesis was not thought through.

## Take profit and stop out

### Principles

- There is one take-profit signal: the market. Hold while the structure holds. Leave when it breaks.
- A stop is an operating cost. Write it before you open. If the other side holds a key level, the old thesis is void.
- Looking back at the price always says "wait, there was more." That is hope. If the trend thesis has changed, leave even when the price is close to the planned level.
- After you flatten, do not immediately reverse, do not regret missing the high, and do not chase ticket after ticket. A reverse needs its own complete thesis and invalidation level. It is not an outlet for the last trade.
- The only judge is structure, not the news and not a later replay of the chart.

### Where key levels come from

- The prior day's low, the overnight low, a gap
- The prior high, a new high, the second-highest high (the second high is the main take-profit level, not necessarily the absolute high)
- Round numbers, and a shelf both sides fought over and then accepted
- The high of an event day, which often becomes resistance afterward
- How far current liquidity can actually carry price, not a fantasy target

Longs: if price keeps losing the shelf, cut or flatten.
Shorts: stop out if the prior resistance or the new high is held the other way. A fresh high that fails at once is a short near that high. The stop is easy to place, and the trade is often better than chasing a short halfway down.
A leader whose relative strength has flipped weak can leave before the index does.

### Write the session tree before the open

At least three branches:

1. The open reclaims and holds the key high → stay in. Add if the trend is intact.
2. The open bounces but cannot reach the prior high or the new high → treat it as the second high. Cut or flatten.
3. The open does not bounce, and price loses the overnight low or yesterday's low → flatten. Do not short immediately after closing a long.

Use the overnight session and the pre-market to revise the tree. Do not invent the story after the regular session starts.

Trend rule: higher highs. While the trend holds, a pullback that then holds a new high can be an entry. It is not "it already went up, so you cannot buy."

Split an event campaign by day: the open, the pullback, the next push, before the event, the close on event day, and after the event. The same kind of event does not print the same candle twice. When the largest piece of good news lands and nothing larger is pulling price, treat it as bad news. When bad news is fully priced, the landing can be a buy.

A trade with a hard time box (a forced outflow, a deal window, a scheduled unlock) can be opened for that window and closed when the window ends. Do not guess the destination inside the window. Stack two or three independent reasons. If you cannot, do nothing.

### Extra requirements for a short

Write all five layers before a short is allowed: the higher-timeframe regime, whether crowding and the good news are already priced, whether capital is moving on a seesaw, whether the chart has a second test or a second high, and the current price plus the invalidation level. Missing one layer means do not short yet.

### A range position

Use a core position in the trend leg. Use a grid to lower cost inside the range. Set a floor. If it breaks, cut the adds or flatten the discretionary book. Only chase after price holds the next round number. If the name turns weak versus the bellwether, cut high-beta exposure first, then decide whether to switch to trading the range.

### Execution

- Size is the user's own capital. Never default to a heavy position or to leverage.
- A probe position is allowed. If it is not strong enough inside the confirmation window (a stretch after the open), take it off.
- An exit can be split: one piece on the impulse, another if the follow-through fails, and one piece left until the structure is done.
- Small capital: each trade has to matter, and it still needs a stop. Large capital: smaller share per trade, fewer trades, and more time once the name is right.

## Output

```
Regime: trend / rotation / range / event campaign
Thesis: one logic + whether price confirms it + what would falsify it
Names: 1–2, why they are strong, their range, the event
Levels: support / resistance / invalidation
Session tree: A / B / C, and what to do if each one fails
Time box: what to do before the next event
Size: how large, how to open, where the stop is
Do not: the middle of the range, weak names, buying the unconfirmed dip, an immediate reverse
Disclaimer: framework sketch, not a promise
```
